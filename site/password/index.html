Password Generator

Random passwords, or passphrases from the EFF word list that are easier to type and remember. Generated by your browser’s cryptographic random number generator; nothing is sent or saved.

    How strength is measured

    Bits of entropy count how many equally likely results these settings can produce: each extra bit doubles the work for an attacker who knows exactly how the password was made. That’s the honest worst case, unlike meters that guess from the password’s appearance.

    Time to crack is the average time to find it at 10 billion guesses a second, roughly a GPU rig attacking a fast, unsalted hash such as MD5 or NTLM. Slow hashes (bcrypt, Argon2) are millions of times slower to attack; online logins with rate limits slower still.

    Rough guide: 64 bits or more for most accounts; 80 or more for anything protected by a fast hash or used as an encryption key, such as a disk, vault or backup password. Six EFF words give about 77.5 bits; seven give about 90.

    The word list is the EFF Large Wordlist (7,776 words, CC BY 3.0 US, by the Electronic Frontier Foundation). Settings are remembered in this browser; generated passwords never are.
