SPF & DMARC Checker

Check the records that stop others sending mail as your domain: SPF (which servers may send), DMARC (what receivers do with failures) and DKIM (signing keys). Look up a domain, or paste a record to check it without any network access.

How SPF, DKIM and DMARC fit together

SPF is a TXT record on your domain listing the servers allowed to send its mail. Receivers check the server that connected against it. Each include:, a, mx, ptr, exists and redirect= costs a DNS lookup, including the ones inside includes, and more than 10 makes SPF fail for all your mail.

DKIM signs each message; the public key is a TXT record at selector._domainkey.example.com. Selectors can’t be listed, so this page tries common ones. The real one is the s= value in a message’s DKIM-Signature header.

DMARC (a TXT record at _dmarc.example.com) tells receivers what to do when a message passes neither SPF nor DKIM for the domain in its From: address, and where to send reports. Start with p=none and a rua= address, read the reports until all your real senders pass, then move to quarantine and reject.

Subdomains without their own _dmarc record use the parent’s. This page finds the parent by removing labels down to two, without the Public Suffix List, so for names under suffixes like co.uk it can look one level too high.
